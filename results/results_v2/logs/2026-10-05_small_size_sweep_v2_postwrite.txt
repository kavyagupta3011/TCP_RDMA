OBSERVATIONS
- The optimization worked: up to ~2.1x faster at small sizes, 1.2-1.8x at larger sizes. At 128 B one-shot
  dropped from 4.28 to 2.08 us, i.e. ~2.2 us saved, consistent with removing one serialized NIC round trip
  (the hypothesis from the v1 sweep).
- Best result so far: 2.08 us for a 128 B one-shot Sentinel AllReduce on 3 ranks.
- Tail latency: medians are tight (p99 within ~10% at 128 B) but there are occasional outliers
  (max ~11-23 us at small sizes, ~88 us for one-shot at 16 KiB), so quote median/p99 alongside the mean.
- The two-shot advantage seen at 64 KiB in v1 (0.85x) is GONE in v2 (1.10x): one-shot gained more from
  overlapping its sends with the wait for incoming data. At N=3 two-shot only saves 1/3 of the traffic
  ((N-1)M vs 2(N-1)M/N), so it may need more ranks or larger messages to win again. Not yet verified.
- Remaining cost at larger sizes (e.g. 64 KiB one-shot 85 us ~ 1.5 GB/s) still looks CPU-bound: the
  per-element volatile sentinel check + reset loop in the reduce. Likely next target.
- Still not compared to the InfiniBand hardware floor (ib_write_lat) -- needed before claiming how close
  this is to a limit. The paper's 1.404 us is an NVLink/GPU figure and not a valid target here.
