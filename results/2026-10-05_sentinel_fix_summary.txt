OBSERVATIONS
- Median is within ~1% of the mean and p99 is only ~13% above it, so the latency is steady, with no
  one-off stall. (The old 57.8 ms Sentinel figure is not reproduced; the original code never re-armed
  the sentinel, so its numbers did not measure a real exchange.)
- Two-shot is now faster than one-shot, as expected for N=3 (it moves less data: 2(N-1)M/N vs (N-1)M floats
  per rank).
- CAVEAT: 256 KiB is a bandwidth-sized message, NOT the small-message latency regime the paper targets
  (paper: ~128 B up to a few KiB). ~500 us here is dominated by moving 256 KiB and by the CPU reduce loop,
  not by synchronization. Not comparable to the paper's ~1.4-5 us numbers.
- Likely remaining costs (hypotheses, not yet measured): rdma_write() is blocking/signaled and posted
  serially per peer; per-element volatile sentinel check + reset in the reduce loop.

NEXT STEPS
1. Rerun at a small message size to see the latency regime, e.g. numFloats = 32, 256, 4096 (128 B, 1 KiB, 16 KiB).
2. Optimize rdma_write (post all writes first, signal only the last, inline small writes) and the reduce loop.
3. Re-run the other algorithms (LL, Twoshot_LL, LL128_Atomic, Baseline_Barrier) with per-iteration timing.
